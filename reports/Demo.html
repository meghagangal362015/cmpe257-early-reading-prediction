Can we spot slow reading growth in the fall?

Teachers need to know early which first graders will fall behind. We train models on what is known at the start of the year and test them on schools they have never seen.

What we found

  • Simple models are enough. Logistic and Linear Regression score as well as Random Forest and XGBoost (about 0.64 accuracy for Word ID, 0.61 for Word Attack).
  • Tuned XGBoost is the best single result: 0.656 accuracy and 0.712 PR-AUC for Word ID.
  • The neural networks did not beat traditional models, and MaskMLP's advantage in the paper was not reproduced: its pretraining collapses every student to the same embedding.
  • The data sets the ceiling. High- and low-growth students are mixed together in feature space (55% same-label neighbors vs 50% by chance), so every model stays near 0.6 to 0.7 ROC AUC.
  • The fall score on the same skill is the strongest input, and removing the reading tests hurts most.

How the label is made

Gain
spring raw score − fall raw score (Woodcock test)
Bar
average gain of the control group (no intervention): 26.32 Word ID, 10.75 Word Attack
Label
high growth if the student's gain is above the bar, otherwise low growth
Balance
Word ID 54% high (383 of 706) · Word Attack 59% high (416 of 706)
Testing
5-fold GroupKFold by school (main) and by student; tuning uses an inner 4-fold split of the training schools only
